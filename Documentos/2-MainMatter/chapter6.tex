\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{cha:conclusion}

This dissertation addressed the engineering bottleneck of creating, configuring, and synchronizing industrial \glspl{DT}. Traditional approaches to virtual commissioning and digital replication rely on fragmented \gls{CAD} files, vendor-locked simulation software, and manual matching of kinematics, sensors, and \gls{IO} addresses. This reliance makes the deployment of \glspl{DT} prohibitively labor-intensive and error-prone. To overcome these limitations, this research proposed, implemented, and empirically validated an open, modular, model-driven framework that procedurally generates interactive 3D simulation environments directly from standardized \gls{AAS} packages without pre-compiled scenes.

This final chapter synthesizes the primary conclusions and technical contributions of the dissertation, discusses the operational limitations identified during testing, and details concrete directions for future research.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusions and Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion_summary}

Guided by the \gls{DSR} methodology, this research delivered a multi-tier pipeline organized across four distinct layers: physical survey and low-polygon asset digitization (Layer~0), model-driven \gls{AAS} authoring and semantic packaging (Layer~1), containerized registry and submodel storage via Eclipse BaSyx v2 (Layer~2), and dynamic procedural runtime instantiation and drift correction in Unity 3D (Layer~3). 

The experimental validation conducted in \Cref{cha:discussion} substantiated the feasibility, determinism, and performance boundaries of this approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fidelity on Mechatronic Scale Hardware (\gls{FMS} Training Bench):} Cold-boot instantiation of the complete modular \gls{FMS} bench (comprising linear, rotational, and transfer conveyors, vertical punch stamping, optical sensors, and physical \gls{PLC} integration) averaged $2.79 \pm 0.04\,$s. In steady state, the physics runtime sustained an average rendering rate of $489.91 \pm 49.21\,$\gls{FPS} with a mean frame latency of $2.36 \pm 0.39\,$ms. The 1\% low frame rate remained above $272\,$\gls{FPS}, and the P99 tail latency stayed below $9.94\,$ms. These metrics confirm that the procedural \gls{DT} provides ample real-time headroom for continuous bidirectional telemetry and visual synchronization.
    \item \textbf{Algorithmic Scalability and Real-Time Limits:} High-density parametric stress testing from $N=5$ to $N=1000$ conveyor modules revealed that the framework scales with near-linear complexity up to 250 modules ($254\,$\gls{FPS} at $20.6\,$s startup time) and retains real-time execution up to 500 modules ($93\,$\gls{FPS}). The empirical evaluation identified single-threaded \gls{CPU} physics resolution as the primary bottleneck beyond 500 modules, establishing clear operational sizing guidelines for model-driven \glspl{DT}.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Control Logic and Virtual Commissioning:} By packaging operational rules within semantic \textit{ControlLogic} submodels, virtual cells operate in pure software \gls{VC} mode without requiring physical hardware. Transitioning to operational \gls{DT} mode requires only redirecting sensor state streams to live \gls{TCP} telemetry from the shop floor, eliminating configuration fragmentation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

The experimental findings presented in this dissertation validate the feasibility of model-driven procedural digital twins for discrete modular transport systems. By demonstrating automated scene generation, sub-3-second startup times, and deterministic multi-hundred FPS synchronization on mechatronic hardware, the framework proves that semantic standards can eliminate manual simulation rebuilds. 

At the same time, the quantitative stress tests and architectural boundaries identified in \Cref{cha:discussion} highlight key challenges that arise when transitioning from controlled pilot benches to large-scale, heterogeneous manufacturing plants. Modern industrial environments operate across multiple physical domains, incorporate articulated robotic manipulators, execute diverse programming logic beyond simple boolean conditions, and require immersive visual interfaces for human operators on the shop floor. 

To bridge these gaps and expand the applicability of the proposed open architecture, five key research trajectories are identified:

\subsection{Physics Acceleration via Unity \gls{DOTS} and Massively Parallel Compute Shaders}
As demonstrated in \Cref{sec:eval_frametime_latency}, scaling beyond 500 active modules exposes the single-threaded \gls{CPU} update boundary of Unity's conventional PhysX implementation. To scale procedural \glspl{DT} to gigafactory density ($N \ge 1000$ modules and tens of thousands of dynamic rigidbodies):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Adoption of Unity \gls{DOTS}:} Transforming asset instances from object-oriented GameObjects into memory-contiguous entities via the \gls{ECS}, the C\# Job System, and the Burst Compiler. This transition allows kinematics, belt velocities, and bounding-box overlap tests to be distributed across all available \gls{CPU} cores.
    \item \textbf{Compute Shader Physics:} Offloading contact-friction calculations and pallet surface kinematics to the \gls{GPU} using compute shaders, enabling real-time execution of massive discrete manufacturing lines without \gls{CPU} saturation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robotic Manipulation Integration via \gls{ROS} and \gls{ROS}~2}
The current framework was benchmarked primarily on material handling and discrete stamping mechanisms. Expanding the pipeline to articulate industrial manipulators and mobile logistics requires standardizing robotic kinematics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\gls{URDF} and Kinematic Tree Ingestion:} Standardizing the encapsulation of \gls{URDF} joint definitions, link mass properties, and transmission limits inside \gls{AAS} submodels.
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional \gls{ROS} / \gls{ROS}~2 Interfaces:} Connecting Unity's \gls{DT} runtime to \gls{ROS}~2 nodes via standardized bridges (e.g., \textit{ROS-TCP-Connector}). This allows motion planners (such as MoveIt~2) to command virtual robotic arms (e.g., Universal Robots UR3e/UR5e) in \gls{VC} mode, with joint state trajectories subsequently verified against physical robot controllers over micro-\gls{ROS} or industrial Ethernet.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Expansion to Continuous and Hybrid Non-Discrete Manufacturing Systems}
The present implementation focuses on discrete manufacturing, where events are triggered by item arrivals and binary sensor states. Many industrial processes operate under continuous or hybrid dynamics (such as chemical batching, fluid flow, thermodynamic heating, and continuous extrusion):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\gls{FMI} Co-Simulation:} Integrating \gls{FMI}/\gls{FMU} standards within the \gls{AAS} ecosystem. Differential equations representing thermal dissipation, pressure dynamics, or material degradation can be solved inside specialized numerical solvers (e.g., OpenModelica, MATLAB/Simulink) while exchanging boundary values cyclically with the 3D physics runtime.
    \item \textbf{Continuous State Submodels:} Specifying volumetric flow rates, temperature fields, and viscosity curves within semantic property hierarchies, bridging discrete kinematics with physical transport equations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extensible Control Logic Interpreters: C, Python, Scratch, and Node-RED}
The rule engine currently parses declarative IF-THEN conditions stored in \gls{JSON} submodels. While effective for simple sequencing, complex manufacturing machinery requires expressive, widely adopted programming paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Native Scripting and Micro-Runtimes (C / Python):} Embedding sandboxed execution engines (such as WebAssembly runtimes or embedded Python/Lua interpreters) directly inside the 3D engine. This allows control engineers to upload procedural control routines written in native C or Python into the \gls{AAS}, which are executed dynamically without modifying the simulation host.
    \item \textbf{Visual and Block-Based Programming (Scratch / Blockly):} Providing educational and rapid-prototyping environments where operators can construct manufacturing sequences using drag-and-drop functional blocks that compile directly into \textit{ControlLogic} submodel schemas.
    \item \textbf{Flow-Based Orchestration via Node-RED:} Supporting external execution engines such as Node-RED. Virtual sensors push \gls{MQTT} telemetry to visual dataflow pipelines, which execute supervisory coordination and dispatch control signals back to virtual actuators in real time.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Immersive Interfaces: Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality (\gls{VR}/\gls{AR})}
Because the runtime engine is developed natively in Unity 3D, extending the procedural pipeline to immersive extended reality headsets (such as Meta Quest, Apple Vision Pro, or Microsoft HoloLens) represents a natural and highly valuable progression:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Immersive \gls{VR} Operator Training and Commissioning:} Allowing maintenance personnel and plant operators to navigate procedurally generated factories at 1:1 scale before physical installation, testing manual interventions, safety zones, and ergonomic clearance without hazards.
    \item \textbf{On-Floor \gls{AR} Digital Overlays:} Superimposing live procedural \gls{DT} data directly onto physical machinery in real time using spatial tracking. Operators equipped with \gls{AR} headsets can visualize operational parameters (such as internal sensor states, pneumatic pressures, kinematic speeds, and active control logic transitions) positioned directly over physical conveyor tracks and actuator enclosures.
    \item \textbf{Remote Collaborative Teleoperation:} Using WebRTC streaming and multi-user networked sessions to allow geographically dispersed automation engineers to inspect anomalous shop-floor events, interact with virtual control panels, and trigger coordinated maintenance routines simultaneously.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, this research confirms that semantic, model-driven architectures can successfully automate the creation and synchronization of industrial \glspl{DT}. By combining open \gls{AAS} standards, modular 3D meshes, and procedural runtimes, the proposed framework eliminates manual scene re-engineering and provides a scalable foundation for next-generation Industry 4.0 smart manufacturing.
